## Comparative implications / candidate hypotheses
H6: Two legitimate but incompatible character goals create changing meaning without adding random visual stimuli. Higher-view pair has stronger examples of independently motivated characters. Test same setting, duration, production quality, conflict vs monologue, repeated topics; retention 5/10/20s, independent audience interpretation.
H7: A payoff that reorganizes an earlier detail (rather than merely increasing scale or punishing predictable villain) may produce recall and sharing. Both comparison videos also have callbacks, so callback alone is not differentiator. Test predictable vs recontextualizing payoff; no guarantee.
H8: Micro-shifts in emotional register (irritation -> trust -> absurdity) may support longer scripts. Contrast to high-stimulation visual sequence without such shifts. Test with content matched and avoid compulsory twists.
H9: Visual stimuli should be tied to a meaningful new action/reaction. Low-view rhythm video has fast, striking motion; cannot equate motion with audience success. Audience retention unknown.
Counterexamples/limitations: four purposively selected items, different story lengths and publishing windows, no own-channel transfer yet, possible automated analysis errors, exposure/format/topic/click choices confounded. Need further comparisons including different creators. No "winning formula" claim.

## Original actor pilot: 求人写真の笑顔
Fictional editorial experiment, not an episode approved for existing series. Keep monochrome atmosphere; temporarily isolate acting/visual story without original closing line, do not change existing canonical ending.
Four beats, 4s each (planned 16s):
1. exhausted office workers switch expressions for a recruitment photograph, then go blank.
2. a job seeker sees the cheerful group photo on a laptop and relaxes.
3. on starting work he encounters the same emotionally exhausted office; his smile fades.
4. he now joins the group photo, learns to switch on a smile himself, returns to blank.
No VO; actor reaction, camera shutter, visual recurrence. Intended visual mechanism: viewers understand the photographed smile is not the experience of employees; job seeker learns the behavior that misled him. Unlike prior block CG, this uses generated film-like shots. It does NOT copy competitor scene details. Main objection: opening reveals discrepancy and payoff is predictable; four cuts may not clearly establish recruitment photo/identity. Treat as an actor/camera diagnostic, not a viral script pass. Alternate if analysis supports: delay first reveal so viewer shares recruit's initial belief, compare against first-frame expression contrast with same body. No claim of real recruitment practice.
